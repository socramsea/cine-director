## THE CANONICAL BLOCK

> A 68 m² two-bedroom apartment on the fourth floor of a low coastal building.
> Interiors in warm white plaster with pale oak flooring in the living areas and
> large-format matte grey porcelain tile in the kitchen and bathroom; window and
> door frames in slim black aluminium; a single continuous eye-level horizon
> line through the living space. Light: mid-morning, roughly 10am, sun high and
> slightly off-frame to the left, daylight-balanced and even, soft shadows with
> no hard edges, every room lit as if photographed within the same twenty
> minutes. Palette: warm white, pale oak, matte grey, black aluminium. Camera:
> wide lens, held at eye height, horizon always level, no tilt, no roll, no
> handheld motion. No people, no pets, no text or graphics, no added or altered
> furniture, no changed views through the windows, no additional light fixtures,
> no reflections of anything not present in the room.

---

## Field-by-field breakdown

| # | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identity of the space | 68 m², 2 bedrooms, 4th floor, coastal building; warm white plaster, pale oak floors, matte grey porcelain in wet areas, slim black aluminium frames |
| 2 | Canonical light | Mid-morning ~10am, sun high and off-frame left, daylight-balanced, even, soft shadows — one condition for the entire property |
| 3 | Frozen palette | Warm white, pale oak, matte grey, black aluminium |
| 4 | Camera signature | Wide lens, eye height, horizon always level, no tilt or roll, no handheld |
| 5 | Explicit prohibitions | No people, pets, text, added or altered furniture, changed window views, added fixtures, or invented reflections |

## Notes on the choices

**The source photo is the identity, not this text.** For a real property, field
1 describes what the photographs show. It does not describe an idealised version
of the apartment. Where the text and the photo disagree, the photo is right and
the text gets corrected — that is the whole difference between a showcase and a
render.

**"Horizon always level" is in the camera signature for a reason.** A crooked
horizon in a property video does not read as a stylistic choice. It reads as a
building that is not straight, which is the single worst thing an architectural
video can accidentally say.

**One light for six rooms, and the rooms were not photographed together.** This
is the field doing the heavy lifting. Real listing photos are taken across an
afternoon, or across two visits in different weather. Freezing the light is what
makes six separately generated shots read as one apartment rather than six
apartments.

**Field 5 is longer than in most character projects.** Every prohibition in it
corresponds to something a generative model reliably adds to an empty interior:
a person for scale, a plant, a warmer lamp, a better view. Each one of those
would make the video prettier and the listing false.
