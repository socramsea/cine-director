Vila Mar 402

A 30-second vertical property showcase, planned shot by shot — and held to what the photographs actually show. No character, no invented rooms, no view the window does not have.

The route

Six shots, in walking order

Entrance, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, balcony, exterior. This is the order someone walks the apartment, not the order that front-loads the best rooms — a showcase that opens on the balcony and closes on the bathroom tells the viewer the bathroom is the disappointment.

The space bible

No character means no character bible. This project uses the five-field space bible instead — and its heaviest field is the light. The source photos were taken across different times of day, as listing photos always are. Freezing one lighting condition is what makes six separately generated shots read as one apartment.

Canonical block — do not edit

A 68 m² two-bedroom apartment on the fourth floor of a low coastal building. Interiors in warm white plaster with pale oak flooring in the living areas and large-format matte grey porcelain tile in the kitchen and bathroom; window and door frames in slim black aluminium; a single continuous eye-level horizon line through the living space. Light: mid-morning, roughly 10am, sun high and slightly off-frame to the left, daylight-balanced and even, soft shadows with no hard edges, every room lit as if photographed within the same twenty minutes. Palette: warm white, pale oak, matte grey, black aluminium. Camera: wide lens, held at eye height, horizon always level, no tilt, no roll, no handheld motion. No people, no pets, no text or graphics, no added or altered furniture, no changed views through the windows, no additional light fixtures, no reflections of anything not present in the room.

Short takes beat one long glide

The decupage

Every shot uses the same card, and every movement is slow. Interiors are where straight lines bend — doors, window frames, skirting boards, tile grids. A five-second take gives the engine less room to drift than a thirty-second one, and the cut hides what remains.

Non-negotiable

The line this does not cross

Movement over what the photograph shows. Nothing added.

The video may generate movement across a real photograph. It may not add, remove or improve anything: no furniture that is not there, no view the window does not have, no room made larger than it is.

This is not a style preference. A showcase of a space that does not exist is deceptive advertising, and the buyer finds out at the viewing. So the prohibition lives inside the space bible itself, in field five, where it travels into every prompt — rather than living in someone's memory.

It is the same rule this whole skill is built on, applied to pixels instead of numbers: do not present something invented as something verified.

One variable, and that is structural

What it costs

No character means no generated reference images — the agent's own listing photographs are the references — and no lipsync pass. Two of the three cost lines a music video carries simply do not exist here.

budget = 45.000 × P_vid

Thirty seconds, times a regeneration margin of 1.5, times today's published price per second. Fill in one number and that is the budget.

The largest decision in it was free: locking 9:16 before the decupage. Generating both 9:16 and 16:9 would have doubled the line — the same six shots, twice — for a second format nobody asked for.

Produced by cine-director in dry-run mode
