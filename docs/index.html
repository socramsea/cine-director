The character bible

The hardest problem in generative video is not image quality. It is the performer becoming a different person between shot 3 and shot 4. This block is the fix: canonical, immutable, never paraphrased to shorten a prompt.

Canonical block — do not edit

A performer in their early thirties, light brown skin, close-cropped black hair, a short full beard kept sharp at the jaw, thick dark eyebrows, a narrow face with high cheekbones. Thin round wire-frame glasses with a faint amber tint, worn in every shot. A single small silver hoop in the left ear, nothing else. Wardrobe: an oversized raw-linen shirt in bone white, sleeves rolled to the forearm, worn open over a plain black tee, and dark indigo trousers. Light: the last twenty minutes of dusk, sun already below the horizon, sky graded from deep teal overhead to a narrow band of burnt orange at the waterline; the performer lit from the front left by that residual warm light, with cool blue fill from the sky behind. Palette: teal, bone white, burnt orange, indigo. Posture: still and grounded, weight on the back foot, chin level, movement economical — small deliberate gestures rather than large ones. No hat, no sunglasses, no visible tattoos, no additional jewelry, no logos or text on the clothing, no other people in frame. Shot on anamorphic glass, shallow depth of field, gentle halation on the highlights, fine 35mm grain.

One action per shot. Two actions means two shots.

The flagged risk

Shot 11 runs longer than anything validated

It is a 10-second crane rise. No take that long has held its geometry on this pipeline, and the safe duration is still an open question. So the shot stays in the decupage at full length — and gets a flag, rather than being quietly shortened to look safe.

If the pilot holds at 10.000 s, it ships as written. If it does not, it splits into two 5-second takes cut on the camera's own movement. The audio does not move. No other shot changes. Both outcomes leave the film at 92.000 seconds — which is exactly why the risk can be carried instead of blocking the lock.

The honest form of an estimate

What it costs

The quantities are exact. The prices are named variables, because generative-video pricing changes without notice and a number frozen into a document is a number that will be wrong by the time you read it. Substitute today's prices and the estimate is correct today.

budget = 18 × P_img  +  138 × P_vid  +  90 × P_lip
Reference images12 → 18 with margin
Video generation92.000 s → 138.000 s
Lip sync60.000 s → 90.000 s
Regeneration margin×1.5 — the pilot, twice, plus rejects

Fill in three numbers and the top line is your budget. Every variable above is a question with an answer you can look up — not a guess dressed as a figure.

Then, and only then

Generate one shot

Shot 03 goes first, alone

It is the most expensive shot to be wrong about: the first close-up with lip sync, framed against moving water, on a performer with a full beard. It carries every unresolved question in the package at once — and it is six seconds long.

If shot 03 passes, the batch is safe. If it fails, it fails for the price of one shot instead of twelve. Generating the batch before the pilot is approved is how a systematic error becomes a systematic error multiplied by twelve.
